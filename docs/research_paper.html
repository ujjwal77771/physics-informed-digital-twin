Abstract—The accurate prediction of remaining useful life (RUL) for industrial rolling element bearings is a cornerstone of intelligent predictive maintenance strategies. While purely data-driven deep learning models have demonstrated significant empirical success, they often lack interpretability and struggle to extrapolate beyond their training distributions, particularly in safety-critical industrial applications subject to varying operational conditions. This paper proposes VibFormer, a novel physics-informed digital twin framework that integrates a specialized Transformer-based architecture with established physical degradation models for bearing prognostics. By embedding Paris-Erdogan fatigue crack growth principles directly into the loss function of a self-attention mechanism, the proposed framework enforces physical consistency without sacrificing the representational capacity of deep learning models. We develop a comprehensive digital twin pipeline that continuously synchronizes sensory vibration data with a virtual counterpart, enabling robust anomaly detection, degradation tracking, and real-time RUL estimation. Extensive experimental validation on the benchmark IMS bearing dataset reveals that VibFormer significantly outperforms conventional prognostic models, including LSTMs and 1D-CNNs, achieving an RMSE reduction of over 18% while enforcing monotonic degradation curves. The framework provides a scalable, generalizable, and physically grounded approach to fleet-wide industrial health management.
Index Terms—Physics-informed neural networks, digital twin, remaining useful life, transformer, bearing prognostics, predictive maintenance.

I. Introduction

In the context of modern Industry 4.0 paradigms, predictive maintenance (PdM) has emerged as a critical capability for minimizing unplanned downtime, reducing maintenance expenditures, and maximizing the operational lifespan of heavy industrial machinery. Among the myriad of mechanical components in industrial systems, rolling element bearings are ubiquitous and arguably the most susceptible to degradation and ultimate failure due to continuous cyclic loading and harsh operating environments. The unexpected catastrophic failure of such bearings often leads to cascading secondary damages, severe production bottlenecks, and substantial financial losses. Consequently, the accurate and reliable prediction of a bearing's Remaining Useful Life (RUL) is an indispensable prerequisite for optimizing maintenance schedules and ensuring overall system reliability.

The evolution of RUL prediction methodologies has broadly transitioned through three distinct phases: physics-based (model-based) approaches, purely data-driven approaches, and more recently, hybrid approaches. Traditional physics-based methods rely on empirical formulations or fundamental physical laws—such as the Paris-Erdogan law for fatigue crack propagation, Taylor's tool life equation, or established wear models—to describe the degradation trajectory. While these models offer unparalleled interpretability and physical consistency, their practical efficacy is frequently compromised by the necessity for precise operational parameters, boundary conditions, and material properties, which are notoriously difficult to estimate in dynamic industrial settings. Furthermore, these models often fail to capture the complex, non-linear interactions inherent in multi-component systems under varying load conditions.

Conversely, the advent of deep learning has catalyzed a paradigm shift towards purely data-driven RUL estimation. Architectures such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and most recently, Transformers, have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in extracting intricate temporal features directly from raw multi-modal sensory data, particularly high-frequency vibration signals. The self-attention mechanism inherent to Transformers is uniquely suited to capture long-range dependencies in sequence data, which is highly advantageous for modelling prolonged degradation processes. However, these models operate largely as "black boxes," lacking transparency and physical grounding. They are inherently prone to overfitting, struggle to generalize across diverse operating domains, and may generate predictions that blatantly violate fundamental physical constraints (e.g., predicting a sudden, unprompted increase in health status, violating the monotonic nature of mechanical degradation).

To bridge the dichotomy between physical rigor and representational flexibility, this research introduces VibFormer, a Physics-Informed Digital Twin (PIDT) framework specifically designed for industrial bearing prognostics. By seamlessly integrating domain-specific physical knowledge into the training schema of a state-of-the-art Transformer architecture, VibFormer leverages the strengths of both paradigms. The integration is achieved via a composite physics-informed loss function that penalizes predictions deviating from established fatigue growth kinetics and monotonic degradation trajectories. Furthermore, this model forms the cognitive core of a broader Digital Twin ecosystem, enabling continuous synchronization between physical assets and their virtual representations.

The main contributions of this paper are multi-fold:

1) We propose the VibFormer architecture, which adapts the Transformer self-attention mechanism specifically for high-dimensional, noisy vibration data inherent to bearing diagnostics.

2) We formulate a novel physics-informed loss function that constraints the deep learning model's output using the Paris-Erdogan fatigue crack growth law, ensuring predictions adhere to physical reality.

3) We design and implement a comprehensive Digital Twin pipeline capable of real-time sensory ingestion, continuous model updating, and fleet-wide asset health monitoring.

4) We conduct rigorous empirical evaluations on the widely benchmarked IMS dataset, demonstrating superior prognostic accuracy and physical consistency compared to conventional state-of-the-art methodologies.

II. Background and Related Work

A. Data-Driven Bearing Prognostics

Data-driven methodologies have dominated recent literature in machine health monitoring. Early approaches primarily utilized shallow machine learning models, such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests, which relied heavily on manual, expert-driven feature engineering. Researchers would extract time-domain statistical features (e.g., root mean square, kurtosis, skewness) and frequency-domain features (e.g., spectral entropy, fault characteristic frequencies) to train these models. The efficacy of such models was thus heavily dependent on the quality of the engineered features, which often failed to capture complex temporal degradation patterns.

The introduction of Deep Learning (DL) circumvented the need for manual feature extraction by automatically learning hierarchical representations from raw or minimally pre-processed data. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been widely employed to process 1D vibration signals or 2D time-frequency representations (e.g., spectrograms) for fault diagnosis and RUL prediction. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), specifically LSTMs and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), subsequently became the de facto standard for RUL estimation due to their inherent ability to model sequential dependencies and time-series forecasting. However, LSTMs suffer from computational inefficiencies during training and struggle to maintain context over extremely long sequences typical of slow bearing degradation.

B. Transformers in Time-Series

Initially conceptualized for Natural Language Processing (NLP), the Transformer architecture has recently been adapted for various time-series applications, including financial forecasting, weather prediction, and industrial prognostics. The core innovation of the Transformer is the multi-head self-attention mechanism, which enables the model to process sequences in parallel and establish direct connections between any two time steps, thereby effectively mitigating the vanishing gradient problem associated with long sequences in RNNs. In the context of bearing prognostics, Transformers can identify complex degradation signatures that span vast temporal distances, correlating early micro-structural changes with ultimate macroscopic failure. Despite their superior feature extraction capabilities, standard Transformers remain purely data-driven and are susceptible to generating physically implausible RUL trajectories.

C. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) represent a groundbreaking synthesis of scientific computing and machine learning. Originally proposed to solve partial differential equations (PDEs), PINNs embed physical governing equations directly into the loss function of a neural network. This acts as a soft constraint, guiding the optimization process toward regions of the parameter space that are consistent with known physical laws.

In prognostics and health management (PHM), the adoption of PINN principles is a nascent but rapidly expanding field. Researchers have attempted to fuse cumulative damage models, kinematic equations, and wear laws with neural networks. This hybridization improves generalization, particularly in data-scarce regimes, and provides a degree of interpretability absent in purely data-driven models. The proposed VibFormer framework extends this paradigm by embedding fracture mechanics principles into the attention mechanism's training objective.

D. Digital Twin Technology in PHM

The Digital Twin (DT) concept involves creating a high-fidelity virtual replica of a physical asset, synchronized through real-time data streams. In industrial settings, DTs facilitate advanced simulation, monitoring, and optimization without interfering with the physical operation. For predictive maintenance, a DT integrates historical data, real-time sensor telemetry, and predictive models to continuously evaluate the asset's health status. The VibFormer framework functions as the predictive engine within such a DT, providing actionable intelligence derived from the continuous stream of vibration data.

III. Problem Formulation

The fundamental objective of prognostic modeling is to estimate the RUL of a physical asset based on a sequence of condition monitoring data collected up to the current time \( t \). Let \( \mathbf{X}_t = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \dots, \mathbf{x}_t\} \) denote a multivariate time-series of sensor observations, where \( \mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d \) represents a \( d \)-dimensional feature vector extracted from raw vibration signals at time step \( i \).

The actual remaining useful life at time \( t \), denoted as \( RUL(t) \), is defined as the duration from the current time to the time of failure, \( T_{failure} \):

$$ RUL(t) = T_{failure} - t \quad (1) $$

In practical implementations, particularly for bearings that operate normally for extended periods before exhibiting measurable degradation, it is customary to employ a piecewise linear degradation model. This approach bounds the maximum predictable RUL to a predefined threshold, \( R_{max} \), acknowledging that predicting failure infinitely far into the future is inherently unreliable. The target RUL is thus defined as:

$$ RUL_{target}(t) = \min(RUL(t), R_{max}) \quad (2) $$

Additionally, we construct a continuous, normalized Health Indicator (HI) bounded between [0, 1], where 1 indicates pristine condition and 0 denotes complete failure:

$$ HI(t) = \frac{RUL_{target}(t)}{R_{max}} \quad (3) $$

The predictive task is to learn a mapping function \( \mathcal{F}_{\theta} \), parameterized by \( \theta \) (representing the neural network weights), such that given the historical sequence of length \( L \), \( \mathbf{X}_{t-L:t} \), the function accurately predicts the future health indicator or RUL: \( \hat{y}_t = \mathcal{F}_{\theta}(\mathbf{X}_{t-L:t}) \), while ensuring that the sequence of predictions \( \{\hat{y}_1, \dots, \hat{y}_t\} \) complies with physical degradation mechanics.

IV. Methodology

A. Physics Foundation: Fatigue Mechanics

The degradation of rolling element bearings is predominantly driven by rolling contact fatigue (RCF), characterized by the initiation and propagation of sub-surface micro-cracks that eventually result in macroscopic spalling. To mathematically ground our predictive model, we rely on established principles of linear elastic fracture mechanics (LEFM). The stress state in the vicinity of a contact surface under Hertzian loading is critical for crack propagation. The Hertzian contact stress, \( \sigma_H \), for a point contact (e.g., in a ball bearing) is approximated by:

$$ \sigma_H = \sqrt{\frac{F}{\pi a b}} \quad (4) $$

where \( F \) is the applied radial load, and \( a \) and \( b \) are the semi-axes of the elliptical contact area, which are functions of the bearing geometry and material elastic moduli.

For cumulative damage assessment, the Palmgren-Miner linear damage rule is often employed, expressing total damage \( D \) as the sum of fractional damages accumulated over varying stress cycles:

$$ D = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{n_i}{N_i} \quad (5) $$

where \( n_i \) is the number of applied cycles at a specific stress level, and \( N_i \) is the fatigue life (total cycles to failure) at that same stress level.

However, to construct a differentiable physical constraint for our neural network, we utilize the Paris-Erdogan law, which models the rate of fatigue crack growth relative to the number of load cycles, \( N \):

$$ \frac{da}{dN} = C(\Delta K)^m \quad (6) $$

where \( a \) represents the characteristic crack length, \( N \) is the cycle number, \( \Delta K \) is the range of the stress intensity factor during a fatigue cycle, and \( C \) and \( m \) are empirical material constants. The stress intensity factor is proportional to the applied stress and the square root of the crack length. In our formulation, the crack length \( a \) serves as a latent analog to our predicted Health Indicator, enforcing a theoretical constraint on the rate of predicted degradation over time.

B. Transformer Architecture

The core predictive engine of VibFormer is predicated on a sequence-to-sequence Transformer architecture, modified to ingest continuous multivariate time-series data rather than discrete tokens.

1) Data Embedding and Positional Encoding: The raw multidimensional sensor data \( \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d} \) is first projected into a higher-dimensional latent space of dimension \( d_{model} \) via a linear feed-forward layer to form the sequence representations:

$$ \mathbf{z}_j = W_{proj} \cdot \mathbf{x}_{(j)} + \mathbf{b}_{proj} \quad (7) $$

Because the self-attention mechanism is inherently permutation-invariant, it lacks the ability to capture sequential order. Therefore, we inject temporal context by adding a learnable positional encoding matrix \( \mathbf{E}_{pos} \) to the projected representations:

$$ \mathbf{Z} = [\mathbf{z}_1, \dots, \mathbf{z}_L] + \mathbf{E}_{pos} \quad (8) $$

2) Scaled Dot-Product Attention: The core computational block maps a query and a set of key-value pairs to an output. The queries \( Q \), keys \( K \), and values \( V \) are obtained via linear transformations of the embedded sequence \( \mathbf{Z} \). The attention function computes compatibility scores and aggregates the values:

$$ \text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V \quad (9) $$

where \( d_k \) is the dimensionality of the key vectors. The scaling factor \( 1/\sqrt{d_k} \) prevents the dot products from growing excessively large, which would push the softmax function into regions with extremely small gradients.

3) Multi-Head Mechanism: To allow the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different temporal positions, VibFormer employs Multi-Head Attention (MHA):

$$ \text{MultiHead}(Q,K,V) = \text{Concat}(head_1,\dots,head_h)W^O \quad (10) $$

where \( head_i = \text{Attention}(QW_i^Q, KW_i^K, VW_i^V) \). This aggregated attention output is subsequently passed through a position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN) containing a non-linear activation, specifically the Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU), to introduce non-linearity and facilitate complex feature mapping:

$$ \text{FFN}(x) = \text{GELU}(xW_1 + b_1)W_2 + b_2 \quad (11) $$

C. Physics-Informed Loss Function

Standard deep learning models are trained using solely data-driven loss metrics, such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), defined over a batch of \( N \) samples:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{data} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2 \quad (12) $$

To impose physical realism, VibFormer augments this empirical risk formulation. First, we translate the Paris-Erdogan law (Eq. 6) into a penalization term. Recall that \( \Delta K = Y \sigma \sqrt{\pi a} \), where \( Y \) is a dimensionless geometry factor. We map the network's predicted health degradation rate \( \dot{\hat{y}} \) to the theoretical crack growth rate \( da/dN \). The physics loss minimizes the discrepancy between the network's finite-difference derivative and the analytical Paris-Erdogan equation:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{physics} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\dot{a}_i - C\left(Y\sigma_i\sqrt{\pi a_i}\right)^m\right)^2 \quad (13) $$

Furthermore, mechanical degradation (absent physical intervention or maintenance) is an irreversible, monotonic process. Data-driven models frequently output highly fluctuating RUL predictions due to signal noise. We introduce a monotonic penalty that applies a heavy loss when the network predicts an increase in RUL over successive time steps:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{mono} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\max(0, \hat{y}_{i+1} - \hat{y}_i)^2 \quad (14) $$

The total loss function utilized during the training phase of VibFormer is a weighted linear combination of these three components, where hyperparameters \( \lambda_1 \) and \( \lambda_2 \) govern the relative strictness of the physical constraints:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \lambda_1\mathcal{L}_{physics} + \lambda_2\mathcal{L}_{mono} \quad (15) $$

D. Digital Twin Pipeline

The theoretical models defined above are integrated into a holistic Digital Twin framework intended for continuous deployment. The physical asset streams high-frequency accelerometer data to edge acquisition nodes. This raw time-domain data undergoes short-time Fourier transform (STFT) to produce comprehensive time-frequency spectrograms, capturing transient impact energies characteristic of localized bearing faults.

These features are ingested by the virtual twin residing in the cloud infrastructure, wherein the VibFormer engine iteratively updates the health state estimation. By aggregating predictions across a fleet of identical assets, the framework dynamically adjusts the empirical constants \( C \) and \( m \) from Equation (6), bridging the gap between theoretical generalized physics and unit-specific operational realities.

V. System Architecture

The system architecture described in this section has been implemented as a functional prototype and validated in a laboratory setting with simulated real-time data streams. The fleet-level monitoring capabilities have been demonstrated with synthetic multi-bearing scenarios; industrial deployment validation with live sensor feeds remains future work.

To ensure scalability and low-latency inference, the proposed architecture follows an Edge-Cloud collaborative topology. Edge devices handle high-frequency sampling (e.g., 20 kHz), signal filtering, and down-sampling to alleviate network bandwidth requirements. The processed sequence features are transmitted via MQTT protocols to a centralized cloud cluster.

The central repository maintains two parallel data stores: a high-throughput time-series database (e.g., InfluxDB) for historical sensor records, and a relational database for asset metadata, maintenance logs, and threshold configurations. The inference engine operates as a containerized microservice, pulling batches of historical context to execute the forward pass of the VibFormer model. The resultant RUL predictions and condition indicators are streamed to operational dashboards, providing maintenance engineers with actionable visualizations and threshold-based automated alerts.

VI. Dataset and Experimental Setup

A. IMS Bearing Dataset Description

The empirical validation of the proposed methodology is conducted using the publicly available dataset provided by the Intelligent Maintenance Systems (IMS) center at the University of Cincinnati. This dataset is widely recognized as a benchmark standard in the PHM community. The experimental test rig involves four Rexnord ZA-2115 double-row bearings mounted on a single shaft, driven by an AC motor at a constant rotational speed of 2,000 RPM. A radial load of 6,000 lbs is applied to the shaft via a spring mechanism, accelerating the fatigue process.

B. Experimental Configurations

The raw vibration signals undergo segmentation with overlapping sliding windows to generate training sequences. We utilize Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) amplitudes across specific frequency bands as our input feature vector \( \mathbf{x}_t \). We employ a leave-one-run-out cross-validation protocol. For each evaluation, one complete run-to-failure trajectory is held out as the test set, while the remaining runs are used for training (80%) and validation (20%). Results are averaged across all three folds and five random seeds (42, 123, 256, 789, 1024). The VibFormer model is implemented using the PyTorch framework. Hyperparameter tuning was conducted via grid search, yielding the optimal architectural parameters outlined in Table II. The model is trained using the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1e-4 and a cosine annealing schedule.

C. Fleet-level Digital Twin Validation

To validate the operational capability of the Digital Twin architecture beyond a single isolated asset, we simulated a fleet of 40 identical bearings operating under slightly varied load conditions. The fleet health heatmap (Fig. 6) visualizes the macroscopic degradation patterns tracked synchronously by the central control system.

To evaluate the generalization performance across disparate operating states, a confusion matrix based on discrete health states (Healthy, Warning, Critical) is constructed for a total of 2,847 test samples across all 3 runs (Fig. 8). The model achieves Precision/Recall/F1 of P=0.992, R=0.981, F1=0.986 for Normal; P=0.924, R=0.949, F1=0.937 for Warning; and P=0.963, R=0.980, F1=0.971 for Critical, resulting in a Macro F1 of 0.965. Notably, there are zero critical-as-normal misclassifications across all test folds, confirming safe failure detection.

D. Sensitivity Analysis of Physics Loss Weights

We conduct a grid search over λ1 ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0} and λ2 ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1} to characterize the sensitivity landscape. Table VII reveals several patterns. First, any nonzero physics weight improves over the pure data-driven baseline (λ1=λ2=0), confirming the value of physics-informed regularization. Second, the RMSE landscape is relatively flat in the range λ1 ∈ [0.05, 0.2], suggesting robustness to moderate perturbations of the selected value. Third, excessive physics regularization (λ1 ≥ 0.5) degrades RMSE by over-constraining the model to match Paris' law residuals at the expense of data fidelity. The monotonicity weight λ2 exhibits a clear trade-off: higher values improve monotonicity scores but beyond 0.05, the diminishing returns in RMSE suggest that moderate enforcement suffices. The selected values (λ1=0.1, λ2=0.05) achieve the best RMSE while maintaining a monotonicity score of 0.97, and were confirmed stable across all five random seeds.

VIII. Discussion

A. Physics-Informed Regularization

The empirical findings strongly corroborate the central hypothesis of this research: embedding phenomenological physics within the learning mechanics of deep neural architectures yields predictive models that are not only quantitatively more accurate but also qualitatively robust. Purely data-driven Transformers, despite their extraordinary capacity to map complex sequence patterns, operate without inherent boundaries; they learn statistics, not physics. When presented with out-of-distribution noise spikes—commonplace in heavy industrial settings due to external operational shocks—standard models often predict temporary "recovery" of the asset, a physical impossibility for fatigued metals.

By intertwining the Paris-Erdogan crack propagation differential equation directly into the gradient descent pathway, VibFormer enforces a reality check on every prediction iteration. The resultant RUL trajectories exhibit a remarkably smooth and monotonic decay, closely mirroring the theoretical expectations of irreversible structural fatigue. The integration of this model into a Digital Twin framework further magnifies its utility, providing a continuously adaptive, fleet-scale monitoring solution that evolves with the physical degradation of the specific assets it oversees.

However, limitations persist. The parameterization of the physical constraints (\( C, m \)) requires careful tuning, and in highly heterogeneous environments where the dominant failure modality shifts away from RCF (e.g., towards chemical corrosion or abrasive wear), the specific Paris law constraint may become suboptimal.

B. Deployment Scope and Limitations

Several limitations warrant explicit acknowledgment. First, the digital twin pipeline has been validated only with the IMS dataset replayed through simulated data streams, not with live industrial sensor feeds. The WebSocket latency and ONNX inference benchmarks (Table VI) were measured on laboratory hardware and may not transfer directly to edge computing environments. Second, the fleet-level monitoring demonstrated in Fig. 6 uses recorded trajectories rather than simultaneous live feeds from multiple bearings. Third, the MQTT/InfluxDB edge-cloud architecture described in Section V represents a reference design validated in Docker containers; production deployment would require additional hardening for network reliability, data integrity, and cybersecurity considerations.

IX. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper introduced VibFormer, a novel Physics-Informed Digital Twin framework engineered to enhance the accuracy and reliability of RUL predictions for industrial bearings. By modifying a sequence-to-sequence Transformer architecture to incorporate fracture mechanics principles via a composite loss function, the model successfully bridges the gap between flexible data-driven learning and rigorous physical bounding. Extensive evaluations on the IMS benchmark dataset validated its superior performance over contemporary DL models, particularly highlighting its capacity to generate physically realistic, monotonic degradation curves.

Future research trajectories will focus on extending the physics-informed paradigm to encompass multi-modal failure mechanisms, integrating thermodynamic and tribological equations to create a unified deterioration model. Additionally, research into dynamic, adaptive weighting of the physics loss term based on real-time uncertainty quantification promises to further elevate the autonomy and robustness of predictive Digital Twins.
